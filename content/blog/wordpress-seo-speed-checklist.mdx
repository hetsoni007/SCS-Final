## Why most WordPress sites are slow (and invisible on Google)

WordPress itself isn't slow — *how it's typically set up* is. The usual culprits stack up quietly: bargain shared hosting, a heavyweight theme, fifteen plugins doing overlapping jobs, full-size images uploaded straight from a phone, and no caching anywhere. Each one costs a few hundred milliseconds; together they produce the 8-second load times that kill both conversions and rankings. Since Google's page-experience signals folded Core Web Vitals into ranking, speed isn't a nice-to-have — it's part of SEO itself.

## The three layers of WordPress performance

### Hosting & server

Everything sits on this. Cheap shared hosting means your site queues behind hundreds of others on the same box. Managed WordPress hosting (₹2,000–₹15,000/month in India) with server-level caching and PHP 8+ is the single highest-leverage upgrade for a slow site — no plugin can compensate for a slow origin.

### Theme & plugins

Every active plugin loads code on every page. Audit ruthlessly: deactivate and delete what you don't use, replace overlapping plugins with one good one, and prefer themes built for performance over ones with 40 bundled sliders. A lean setup is also a smaller attack surface — fewer things to exploit and fewer updates to miss.

### Assets & delivery

Images are usually the heaviest thing on the page: serve WebP, size images to their display dimensions, and lazy-load below the fold. A caching plugin (page + browser caching) plus a CDN puts your content physically closer to visitors — which matters across India's variable mobile networks more than almost anywhere.

<Callout>

**Rule of thumb:** if your homepage weighs more than ~1.5–2 MB or takes more than 3 seconds on a mid-range phone over 4G, you're losing real customers — Indian mobile traffic is the majority of most local businesses' visits, and patience on mobile is short.

## The SEO half of the checklist

Speed gets you crawled and ranked *fairly*; on-page SEO gets you ranked *at all*. The non-negotiables: a proper SEO plugin (or clean manual setup) generating titles, meta descriptions and an XML sitemap; schema markup for your business type; HTTPS everywhere; and — the one everyone forgets — **redirects that preserve your old URLs** when a site is rebuilt or migrated. A redesign that drops old URLs without 301s can throw away years of accumulated ranking in a week.

Content structure matters too: one clear H1 per page, descriptive headings, internal links between related pages, and alt text on images. None of it is exotic — it's discipline, applied consistently. (It's the same discipline we apply to our own site, which is how this page found you.)

## The India-specific speed reality

Global speed advice assumes fast networks and new phones. India's reality: the majority of your visitors are on mid-range Android devices over variable 4G, where a site that feels "fine" on your office WiFi takes 8+ seconds. Two practical consequences — test on real mid-range hardware over mobile data, not just Lighthouse on a laptop; and weight your effort toward the things that help constrained networks most: aggressive image optimisation, a CDN with Indian edge locations, and shipping less JavaScript in the first place.

## Speed and SEO are the same project

Teams often budget "speed optimisation" and "SEO" as separate line items. In practice they're one discipline: Core Web Vitals are literally a ranking input, crawl budget favours fast sites, and bounce-backs from slow pages feed negative engagement signals. ## Frequently asked questions

### Why is my WordPress site so slow?

Usually a stack of small causes: cheap shared hosting, a heavy theme, too many plugins, unoptimised images and no caching. Hosting and caching are typically the biggest wins — fix those first.

### Does site speed really affect Google rankings?

Yes — Core Web Vitals are part of Google's page-experience ranking signals, and slow pages also convert worse even when they do rank. Speed is both an SEO and a revenue issue.

### How many plugins is too many?

There's no magic number — 15 lean, well-built plugins beat 5 bloated ones. The real rule: every active plugin must earn its place, and overlapping plugins should be consolidated.

### Will a rebuild hurt my existing Google rankings?

Only if it's done carelessly. Preserving URL structure — or 301-redirecting every old URL to its new equivalent — carries your rankings across. Skipping that step is the most common self-inflicted SEO disaster.
