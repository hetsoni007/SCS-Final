## The method behind every app cost estimator

Strip away the interface and an app cost estimator does one thing: it predicts **how many weeks of senior engineering your app needs**, then multiplies by a **blended weekly rate**.

That is it. Design, development, testing and project management are all folded into the weeks. The rate covers the team that delivers them. The reason two estimates for the "same" app can differ by 3x is almost always a difference in assumed weeks, not in rates.

The estimator on our [app cost calculator](/app-cost-calculator/) works exactly this way, and we publish its logic so you can check it:

-   **Base weeks by stage:** about 4 weeks for an MVP, 8 for a market-ready product, 14 for a scale or enterprise build.
-   **Feature weeks added on top:** for example user accounts about 1.5 weeks, payments about 2.5, chat about 2.5, maps about 2, push notifications about 1, an admin dashboard about 3, AI integration about 3.
-   **Backend add-on:** none for a managed backend, about 3 weeks for a custom API, about 5 for real-time features at scale.
-   **Multipliers:** iOS-only or Android-only trims about 10 percent; custom UI adds about 20 percent; premium animated design adds about 40 percent.
-   **Rate:** an indicative blended range of $360 to $600 per week.

## The five inputs that move the number most

### 1. Stage and ambition

Proving one idea is not the same job as running a business on the product. The MVP base is about 4 weeks of work; the scale base is about 14 before a single feature is added. Most overspending starts with building the scale version when you need the MVP.

### 2. Features, and especially the expensive ones

Accounts and push notifications are cheap. Payments, chat, maps, offline sync and AI each cost real weeks, because each has edge cases that only show up in production. Our [MVP tech stack guide](/blog/mvp-tech-stack-2026) covers which of these to buy rather than build.

### 3. Backend

A managed backend is the cheapest start. A custom API gives you control and costs about three more weeks. Real-time at scale is the most expensive tier. Pick the lowest tier your first year genuinely needs.

### 4. Platforms

A single React Native codebase serves iOS and Android together, so you are not paying twice. Launching on one platform first trims only about a tenth, so the real saving is sharing one codebase rather than building two apps. We compare the trade-offs in [native vs cross-platform in 2026](/blog/native-vs-cross-platform-2026).

### 5. Design depth

Standard components are fastest. Brand-led custom design adds about a fifth. Heavy animation adds about two fifths. Spend design effort where users feel it, usually onboarding and the core flow.

## What an estimate leaves out

A good estimate is honest about its edges. Plan separately for:

-   **Maintenance:** roughly 15 to 20 percent of build cost per year. See [what app maintenance really costs](/blog/app-maintenance-cost).
-   **Third-party fees:** payment processors, SMS, maps and AI usage scale with your users.
-   **Store fees and accounts:** small, but not zero.
-   **Marketing and content:** an app nobody hears about is the most expensive kind.

## How to sanity-check any estimate

1.  **Ask for the weeks.** If a quote cannot be broken into weeks by feature, it is a guess, not an estimate.
2.  **Compare on the same scope.** Write the feature list first, then ask each vendor to price that list.
3.  **Look for a range, not a point.** Early estimates should be a range that narrows after discovery.
4.  **Beware of the cheapest.** A rebuild costs more than doing it properly once.
5.  **Cross-check with a tool.** Run your scope through an independent estimator. If two methods land within about 30 percent of each other, you are in the right neighbourhood.

## Frequently asked questions

### How accurate is an app cost estimator?

Indicative, not binding. A well-built estimator is typically within a reasonable band of the final number when your scope is clear, and wider when it is not. Treat the output as a planning range and confirm it with a discovery call.

### How do I estimate the cost of an app myself?

List your features, assign each a number of weeks, add backend and design adjustments, then multiply the total by a blended weekly rate. The calculator on this site follows that same method.

### Why do app estimates vary so much between agencies?

Because they assume different scope, different team seniority and different rates. Compare quotes against the same written feature list and ask for the weeks behind each number.

### Is a free app cost calculator reliable?

It is a good first pass if it shows its assumptions. Be cautious of any tool that returns a single exact price from a handful of questions without explaining how it got there.

### What is the cheapest way to build an app?

Build a true MVP: one core loop, a managed backend, standard design and a single codebase for both platforms. Then add features as real users ask for them.
