## Indicative Android app cost

These ranges use the method behind our [app cost calculator](/app-cost-calculator/): weeks of senior engineering multiplied by an indicative blended rate of $1,800 to $3,200 per week. They are planning ranges, not quotes.

| Android-only app | Scope | Weeks | Indicative range |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lean MVP | accounts, push notifications, managed backend | about 6 | $11k to $19k |
| MVP with payments | the above plus payments | about 8 | $14k to $26k |
| Market-ready | payments, admin dashboard, analytics, custom API, custom UI | about 22 | $40k to $70k |

## What Android-only really saves

In our model, building for one platform trims about **10 percent** against building for both from a shared codebase. The market-ready example above is about 22 weeks on Android alone and about 25 weeks for iOS and Android together.

The saving is small because most of the work is shared: the backend, the business logic, the design and the testing of flows. The second platform adds comparatively little when both come from one React Native codebase. We explain the trade-off in [native vs cross-platform](/blog/native-vs-cross-platform-2026).

So choose Android-only for a product reason, not mainly to save money:

-   Your users are overwhelmingly on Android.
-   You need hardware or system features specific to Android.
-   You want one store's feedback before widening the launch.

## What drives Android cost

### Device range

Android runs on many screen sizes, chipsets and OS versions. Supporting older and lower-end devices increases testing and performance work.

### Features

Payments, chat, maps, offline sync and AI each add weeks. The [app cost estimator guide](/blog/app-cost-estimator) lists typical figures.

### Backend

A managed backend is cheapest. A custom API adds about three weeks, and real-time at scale about five.

### Design

Standard components are quickest. Custom UI adds about a fifth, and premium animated design about two fifths.

### Play Store requirements

Store policies, data-safety declarations and testing requirements take time before launch. Our [launch checklist](/blog/app-store-launch-checklist) covers them.

## Native Android or cross-platform?

Native Kotlin makes sense when the app depends heavily on Android-specific capabilities. For most business apps, a cross-platform codebase gives you Android now and iOS later with little extra work. See [Kotlin Multiplatform vs React Native](/blog/kotlin-multiplatform-vs-react-native) and [React Native vs Flutter](/blog/react-native-vs-flutter-2026).

## Costs after launch

Plan for maintenance of roughly 15 to 20 percent of build cost per year, as new Android versions and library updates arrive, plus hosting and third-party fees. See [app maintenance cost](/blog/app-maintenance-cost).

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does it cost to build an Android app in 2026?

Using our method, a lean Android MVP is about 6 weeks and roughly $11k to $19k, and a market-ready Android app with payments, an admin dashboard and a custom backend is about 22 weeks and roughly $40k to $70k. These are indicative ranges.

### Is it cheaper to build only for Android?

Slightly. In our model a single platform is about 10 percent less than building both from one shared codebase, because most of the work is shared.

### Should I build native Android or cross-platform?

Choose native when the app relies on Android-specific capabilities. For most business apps a cross-platform codebase is more economical and makes adding iOS later straightforward.

### What makes Android apps more expensive to test?

The range of devices, screen sizes and OS versions. Supporting older and lower-end phones adds testing and performance work.

### How long does it take to build an Android app?

About 6 weeks for a lean MVP and about 22 weeks for a market-ready product in our examples, depending on features, backend and design.
